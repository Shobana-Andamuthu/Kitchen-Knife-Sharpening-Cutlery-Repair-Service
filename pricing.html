Thinning & Timber Care

Geometry Thinning & Handle Repairs

Repeated sharpening thickens blade shoulders, causing wedging in food. Thinning restores effortless cuts. Loose handles and dry timber are re-pinned and waxed.

  • • Primary bevel thinning & satin scratch reset
  • • Wa-handle replacement, re-gluing, and ferrule alignment
  • • Western scale rivet tightening & beeswax conditioning

Fair Bench Billing

What Affects the Price?

Every knife has a different history. Five core factors determine the bench time, stone wear, and care needed.

Bench Transparency
“Pricing is based on what the knife actually needs to perform safely—no surprise fees, just honest bench work.”
  • ✓ Hand waterstones only — zero motorized heat
  • ✓ Written quote confirmed before any sharpening begins
  • ✓ Edge angle documented for consistent future sharpening
01

Blade Type & Steel

High-carbon Japanese steels (62–66 HRC) require diamond and ceramic stones. Softer European steels require different stone grits to avoid burr folding.

02

Blade Condition

Routine dullness takes standard waterstone progression. Blades damaged by pull-through sharpeners have dished bellies that must be reset from scratch.

03

Damage Severity

Minor dullness removes only microns of steel. Deep chips or broken tips require grinding back millimeters across the belly to keep the rocking curve balanced.

04

Repair Complexity

Thick blades need primary bevel thinning so they won't wedge in vegetables. Re-polishing kasumi satin finishes adds skilled bench hours.

05

Work Required

Total bench time: coarse shaping, progressive stones up to 8000-grit, stropping, bolster buffing, and organic camellia oil sealing.
